4D Compression Leggings: How They Actually Work

We make these, so we will be precise about them. What the knit does, what the research supports, what our garment is made of, and the claims we will not make.

Published September 17, 2026 · 8 minute read
In one paragraph

4D compression is a knitted garment that combines graduated compression, firmest at the ankle and easing as it travels up the leg, with a raised three dimensional knit structure whose micro massage effect is activated by your movement. The fourth dimension is time. A smooth compression stocking applies broadly the same pressure whether you are sitting or walking. A raised 4D knit deforms as the leg moves underneath it, so the pattern of pressure against your skin changes continuously through the day rather than staying static.

"4D" is a descriptive label, not a regulated compression class. No standards body certifies a garment as 4D. The two mechanisms it describes are real and separately documented, and the rest of this page shows the evidence for each, along with the limits.

Why our leggings say 3D and this page says 4D

Worth clearing up first, because it looks like an inconsistency and it is actually the whole mechanism.

3D describes the knit. The fabric is knitted with a raised, three dimensional surface structure rather than as a smooth flat face. That structure is physically there whether you are moving or not, and you can feel it with a fingertip.

4D describes what that knit does over time. In textile engineering, a 4D textile is one whose behaviour changes after it is knitted in response to a stimulus. Research labs usually use heat or electricity as the trigger. In compression legwear the trigger is you. Every step loads and releases the raised structure against your skin, which is why this category of garment does most of its work while you are walking rather than while you are sitting still.

So the honest translation of the whole label is: a three dimensional knit whose effect is movement activated. Our product names say 3D because that is the fabric. This page says 4D because that is the mechanism.

The graduated part, and the research behind it

Graduated compression means the garment is firmest at the ankle and progressively lighter as it moves up the calf, knee and thigh. The mechanism is well described: graduated pressure narrows the diameter of the superficial veins, which increases the velocity of blood returning toward the heart, and supports the calf muscle pump that does that work when you walk.

The useful thing about the published research is that the benefits show up at light pressures, not just at prescription strength.

That last figure is the reason this product exists. Prescribed compression works. Somewhere between a third and two thirds of the people given it stop wearing it, because it is hot, hard to pull on, and looks like a medical device. We are not trying to out pressure a prescription garment. We are trying to be the pair that actually gets worn on the days the prescribed one stays in the drawer.

The micro massage knit

The raised structure is the second half. The honest description of what it does is mechanical rather than medical: a three dimensional surface under pressure creates many small contact points instead of one smooth contact plane, and those points shift, load and release against the skin as the limb moves. You can feel it. It is a light kneading sensation while you walk, and it fades to almost nothing when you sit still, which is exactly what you would expect from a movement activated design.

This is not a category we invented. Micromassage fabric is a real term from Italian medical hosiery manufacturing, where a patented wave structure knit has been built on this principle for decades, and those manufacturers are explicit that their garments give their best when the body is in motion.

There is one piece of published clinical evidence for this garment type, and we would rather state it precisely than stretch it. In 2024, researchers published a pilot study in the journal Life in which 29 women with lipedema wore light micromassage compression leggings rated 15/21 mmHg for around three hours a day for four weeks, alongside exercise. Evoked lower body pain scores fell from 3.03 to 0.56, and average limb volume fell about 7.1%.

What that study does not say

It was a pilot: 29 women, no control group, four weeks, combined with exercise, and the authors say the results need confirmation. It tested one specific Italian garment with a lab measured pressure rating. It did not test our leggings. We cite it because it is the best published evidence that a comfort grade micromassage legging can relieve symptoms, not as evidence about our product.

Why there is no mmHg number on this page

A real compression rating comes from a lab pressure profile measured on the actual garment. We have not had ours tested yet, so we do not publish a figure, and we do not describe these as medical grade, because that term points at measured pressures of 20 to 30 mmHg and above. Any brand in this category quoting a pressure rating should be able to tell you who measured it. Plenty cannot.

The claims we will not make

Most of what gets this category called a scam comes from a short list of claims repeated across dozens of near identical storefronts. For the record, here is where we stop.

  • We do not say these remove or reduce cellulite. Cellulite is structural. Fat lobules press through fibrous bands under the skin, and those bands tether the surface into dimples. No garment changes that. Compression and a dense opaque knit genuinely smooth and even how legs look while the leggings are on, and that effect ends when they come off. Around 80 to 90% of women past puberty have cellulite. It is normal anatomy.
  • We do not quote a circulation percentage. You will see "boosts circulation by 30%" across this category. There is no published figure behind it. The mechanism is real and the number is invented.
  • We do not say medical grade. Our garment is not pressure rated or registered.
  • We do not say these treat or cure lipedema or lymphoedema. Compression is a genuine pillar of conservative lipedema care and the 2024 S2k guideline is clear that the aim is relief of symptoms, particularly pain. Management is not cure. If you are diagnosed, fitted flat knit compression from your clinician stays the standard, and ours is a companion for the hours in between, not a replacement.
  • We do not say these tighten loose skin. After major weight loss, stretched collagen and elastin do not fully rebound. What compression genuinely does is hold and support the skin so it moves with you, and reduce skin on skin friction and chafing.
  • We do not tell you to sleep in them. A movement activated garment does nothing while you are lying still. Pull them on in the morning, take them off in the evening.
  • We do not say the compression lasts forever. Elastane fatigues. Three to six months is honest.

Why we call it Japanese

Two reasons, and one line we are careful about.

The engineering. Seamless three dimensional knitting is a Japanese invention. Shima Seiki, based in Wakayama, built the first machine able to knit a complete garment in three dimensions without seams and showed it in 1995. The company holds a patent portfolio of more than 2,900 filings. Japan is also home to Asahi Kasei, whose Roica elastane is used across performance legwear.

The culture. In Japan, compression legwear is an ordinary everyday product rather than a medical one. It is sold as chakuatsu legwear in mainstream drugstore chains alongside cosmetics, from brands like Slim Walk and MediQtto, and it is bought for mukumi, the everyday word for the leg swelling and heaviness that builds through the day. English has no single word for that, which is part of why nobody here was ever sold a daily fix for it.

The careful line. Japanese 4D compression describes the approach we build to: everyday, appearance led, comfort grade compression of the kind Japanese women have worn for years. It is not a claim about where our garment is manufactured, and we do not make one.

Common questions

Is 4D compression a real thing or just marketing?

The label is marketing. There is no 4D compression standard and no body that certifies a garment as 4D. The construction it describes is real: a graduated compression garment with a raised three dimensional knit whose effect is activated by movement. Both of those mechanisms are documented. Judge a brand on whether it will tell you its fibre composition and pressure rating, not on how many dimensions it claims.

What is the difference between 3D and 4D compression leggings?

In practice they usually describe the same garment. 3D refers to the raised three dimensional structure of the knit itself. 4D refers to what that structure does over time, meaning the massage effect that is produced by your movement rather than applied statically. Neither term is standardised.

What compression level are Sira Sculpt leggings?

They are comfort grade. We do not publish a mmHg figure because we have not had the garment lab pressure tested, and we would rather say that than print a number we cannot stand behind. We also do not describe them as medical grade, which refers to measured pressures of 20 to 30 mmHg and above.

Will 4D leggings get rid of my cellulite?

No. Cellulite is fibrous bands tethering the skin over herniating fat lobules, and no garment changes that structure. Compression with a dense opaque knit smooths and evens how skin looks while the leggings are worn, and the effect ends when you take them off.

Can I wear these instead of my prescribed compression?

No. If you have been prescribed a compression class or fitted for flat knit garments, keep using them, particularly during a flare. These are designed as the comfortable everyday layer for the hours in between, not a replacement for medical compression.

Can I sleep in them?

No. Daytime compression is designed for an upright, moving body, and a movement activated knit does nothing while you are lying still. On in the morning, off in the evening.

How long will they last?

Three to six months of regular wear before the compression noticeably fades, which is the same replacement interval the medical hosiery industry uses. Elastane loses its recovery over time and heat speeds that up, so wash cold and air dry rather than tumble drying.

What are they made of?

90% polyamide and 10% elastane, circular knitted with a raised three dimensional surface structure. The polyamide gives durability and a smooth hand, the elastane gives the recovery that lets the garment hold its shape and its pressure through movement.
